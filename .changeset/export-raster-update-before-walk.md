A browser export now draws the frame right after a scroll or a layout change with what the engine takes at its next rendering update (scroll-state container queries, anchor positions and their fallbacks, `content-visibility: auto`, scroll and view timelines), as `swarmloom render` does. That frame used to show this state from before or after the update depending on timing, so on a page with a scroll-state sticky header the go-live frame could show the header unstuck. On a page whose CSS uses one of those (in a style sheet that applies or a `style` attribute, or a style sheet of another origin, whose rules cannot be read; not in a sheet switched off or for other media, as the Studio sheets its export frame mutes), a frame that walks after a change now waits for that update first: up to a display frame, about 4 to 8 ms a walk in headless Chromium at 1280x720, so such an export takes about 7 to 19% longer where most frames walk, as with a spinner or a live tail, with the same frames. Other pages, `examples/markdown/welcome.zh.md` and `examples/web/demo.html` among them, do not wait. In a hidden tab, which takes no rendering updates, frames do not wait and keep that state as the engine last took it.
